abstract: "Vision-language models (VLMs) learn strong statistical priors during training that can make them fail to perceive visual evidence when an image violates those priors—failures often misread as missing visual capability rather than a learned bias. Using controlled visual in-context learning, we show that demonstrations let VLMs overcome these priors: across five vision-centric tasks and five model families, they yield large gains on prior-conflicting counterfactual examples (up to +30.3%) while leaving real accuracy nearly unchanged. These results reveal that current VLMs possess visual grounding capabilities that standard evaluations overlook, underscoring the need for controlled evaluation design."
